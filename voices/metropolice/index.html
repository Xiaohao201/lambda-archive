Metropolice voice lines — Half-Life 2, by situation

Voice archiveHalf-Life 2Updated 2026

The Metropolice — Civil Protection, CP, metrocops — are the human face of Combine rule in City 17, and almost everything you understand about that rule in the first twenty minutes comes through their voice lines rather than any exposition.

Below are the lines grouped by what triggers them, followed by the part nobody explains: the radio codes are real. Valve didn't invent the jargon.

The can

The most quoted exchange in the game, and it does nothing.

The sequence

  1. "Pick up that can."
  2. "I said pick up the can."
  3. "Pick. Up. The can."
  4. "Now, put it in the trash can."

No door opens. No path unlocks. Refuse and you get hit; comply and he lets you past — the same outcome either way. Notice the third line: each word separated into its own sentence. That's not a delivery quirk, it's written that way, and it's the moment the exchange stops being an instruction and becomes a demonstration.

It teaches you the rules of City 17 without a single line of narration: this person can make you do pointless things, and there is nothing you can do about it. Every player who's ever complained about being made to pick up the can has learned exactly what the scene set out to teach.

The radio codes, decoded

This is the detail that makes the Civil Protection radio chatter land, and it's almost never explained: these are real American police radio codes, used correctly.

CodeReal-world meaningIn Half-Life 2
10-8 In service, available for assignment — a standard ten-code A unit reporting itself on duty and ready. Ambient, not directed at you.
11-99 Officer needs immediate assistance — a California Highway Patrol code, and about the most serious call on the radio Called when you've become an active threat. One of the clearest audio markers that a fight has fully escalated.
Code 3 Emergency response — lights and siren, proceed immediately Units are being ordered to converge at speed rather than patrol pace.

Choosing real codes instead of invented ones is why Civil Protection sounds like a police force rather than a science-fiction army. Combine that with the alien terminology layered on top — the location prefixes, the numbered unit designations — and you get the thing the game is actually about: a recognisably human institution wearing something else's uniform.

Metropolice vs the Overwatch Voice

These get conflated constantly, and they're different voice sets:

MetropoliceOverwatch Voice
WhoHuman officers on the streetSynthesised announcer
SoundMale, heavily radio-filteredFemale, synthetic, clipped
WhereWherever the officers arePublic address and Combine radio
AddressesYou, directlyThe units and the population, about you

If the line is a woman assembling stilted sentences out of fragments, it's Overwatch. If it's a man telling you to pick up a can, it's a metrocop.

Why they sound like that

Every Metropolice line is processed through a radio filter — band-limited, compressed, with the top and bottom of the frequency range cut away, the way a real handheld radio sounds.

It does two things at once. It hides the fact that a small number of voice actors are playing an entire police force, because heavy filtering flattens individual vocal character. And it removes the humanity from the delivery — you never hear a metrocop's actual voice, only the version the equipment lets through. The masks cover their faces; the filter covers everything else.

Frequently asked

Can I download the Metropolice sound files?

Not from us — the audio is Valve's copyrighted work and we don't host or redistribute game assets. The files ship inside your own installation of Half-Life 2, and Valve's own tools are the intended way to browse them.

Is this every line in the game?

No. This covers the lines you'll actually encounter, grouped by trigger. The full voice set includes further variants and situational barks, and the game's own files are the complete reference.

Why do they call Gordon "that guy"?

Because at that point most of them don't know who he is. "You're that guy!" and "Isn't that Freeman?" are recognition lines — the uncertainty is deliberate, and it shifts as the game progresses and you become an actual priority.

Do the Metropolice have unique names or designations?

Individually, no. Civil Protection units are identified by code names and numbers rather than names — one of the ways the game strips them of individual identity.
