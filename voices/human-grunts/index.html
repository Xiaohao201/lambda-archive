Human Grunt voice lines — Half-Life's HECU soldiers

Voice archiveHalf-Life 1Updated 2026

When the marines arrive at Black Mesa, most players' first reaction is relief. Help has come. That reaction survives about four minutes, and it isn't a cutscene that kills it — it's a line of radio chatter you overhear from around a corner.

The line: "I killed twelve dumbass scientists and not one fought back. This sucks."

The HECU — Hazardous Environment Combat Unit, called Human Grunts in the game's own files — were never a rescue. Half-Life tells you this without a single line of exposition.

The line that reframes the game

"I killed twelve dumbass scientists and not one fought back. This sucks."

Every clause is doing damage:

  • "I killed twelve" — not a firefight. A count.
  • "scientists" — the people you've spent the game escorting to retinal scanners.
  • "not one fought back" — they weren't combatants and everyone involved knew it.
  • "This sucks." — the part that lands hardest. He isn't horrified. He's bored. This is a shift he's working.

Half-Life never tells you the military's orders. It doesn't need to. One overheard radio line establishes that the containment operation includes the witnesses, and it does so in the voice of someone finding the job tedious.

It also retroactively changes the scientists. Every "I'll wait right here" you walked away from becomes something else once you know what's coming down the corridor behind you.

They know your name

"So who is this guy Freeman?"
"They say he was at ground zero."

Two lines of idle radio chatter, and the entire status of the protagonist changes.

Note the asymmetry in how they talk. Aliens are "bogies", "hostiles", "movement" — unidentified contacts. You are "Freeman". A named human being, discussed by soldiers who have been briefed about you and are curious.

This is the moment Gordon stops being a survivor and becomes a target with a file. And like everything else in this game, it happens in dialogue you might walk past.

AD SLOT · ARTICLE 02

Real radio procedure

The HECU sound like a military unit because they use military radio conventions correctly:

  • "over" — closing a transmission, used properly rather than decoratively.
  • "sir" — there's a chain of command above them that you never meet.
  • "bogies" — genuine slang for unidentified contacts.
  • "sector secure" / "no movement this level" — structured status reporting.

This is the same technique the sequel uses on Civil Protection, whose radio codes — 10-8, 11-99, Code 3 — are real American police codes. In both games, an institution becomes believable because it speaks the way real institutions speak.

The pattern across the series: the scientists sound like colleagues, the marines sound like a unit, the Metropolice sound like a police force, and the citizens sound like a population. None of it is explained; all of it is conveyed through how people talk on the radio.

Frequently asked

Why are they called Human Grunts?

It's the internal asset name in Half-Life's files, which is why the term shows up in modding and community discussion. In the fiction they're the HECU — Hazardous Environment Combat Unit.

Do the HECU fight the aliens too?

Yes, and it's one of the best things in the game. Three-way fights let you sit back and let two enemy factions reduce each other first.

Are the HECU in Opposing Force?

Opposing Force puts you on the other side of this — you play a HECU soldier. It recontextualises these same lines considerably.

Is this every HECU line?

No. This covers the lines you'll actually hear, grouped by trigger. The game's own files are the complete reference.
