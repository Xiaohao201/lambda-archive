Half-Life voice line archive

Section indexGoldSrc & SourceUpdated 2026

Half-Life tells you almost nothing directly. There are no cutscenes in the first game and no cutaways in the second — everything you learn about Black Mesa, the Combine and City 17 arrives through what people say near you while you're doing something else.

That makes the voice lines the actual script. This section transcribes them and, more usefully, records what triggers each one — because a line means something different depending on whether it fires when you're spotted, when you're hiding, or when they've decided you're worth killing.

How the two engines handle speech

The reason Half-Life 1 and Half-Life 2 NPCs sound so different isn't just better voice acting — it's a different technical approach.

Half-Life 1 (GoldSrc)Half-Life 2 (Source)
ApproachShort fragments assembled at runtimeComplete recorded lines, selected by rules
ResultClipped, stitched rhythmNatural delivery, full performances
StrengthEnormous coverage from few recordingsCharacters who sound like people
WeaknessAudibly assembledEvery variation must be recorded

This is why Half-Life 1's scientists have that distinctive halting cadence. It isn't bad acting — it's a small library of fragments being recombined to cover far more situations than were ever recorded whole. Half-Life 2 could afford complete performances, and the difference in how alive the world feels is largely down to that.

Where the audio is, and why it isn't here

We don't host game audio. Voice lines are Valve's copyrighted work. This site publishes transcripts and analysis — the words, the triggers, the context — and not the recordings.

The files are already on your machine if you own the games, and Valve's own development tools are the intended way to browse them. Anything offering bulk downloads of Half-Life voice audio is redistributing Valve's assets, whatever it says about itself.

Frequently asked

Are these transcripts complete?

They cover the lines you'll actually encounter, grouped by trigger. Voice sets contain further variants and rare situational barks; the games' own files are the only complete reference and we say so on each page rather than claiming totals we can't verify.

Can I use these transcripts in my own project?

The dialogue is Valve's. Our contribution is the organisation, trigger context and commentary. Quote what's useful, credit where it came from, and don't present Valve's writing as your own.

Which character will you archive next?

Based on what people are actually looking for — the Black Mesa scientists, the Human Grunts, and the Citizen voice sets are the ones most searched for. Tell us if there's one you want.

Do mods change these lines?

Some replace voice sets entirely. Anything shipping its own sound folder can override what you hear — see the mod guides.
