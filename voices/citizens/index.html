Half-Life 2 citizens — what "Female 01" is, and what they say

Voice archiveHalf-Life 2Updated 2026

Female 01 is not a character. It's an asset name — one of the citizen voice sets Half-Life 2 draws from when it populates City 17. If you arrived here looking for who she is, that's the answer, and the more interesting answer is why the game is built that way.

How the citizen system works

Citizens aren't authored individually. The game has a pool of models and a pool of voice sets, and NPCs get assembled from them — nine male models and six female, in varying states of dress and wear.

female_01 is one entry in that pool. It has recorded audio, a matching model variant, and no name, no biography and no arc.

That anonymity is the design. City 17's population is meant to read as a population — interchangeable, processed, relocated. Giving each citizen an identity would undercut the entire premise. The people who do get names — Alyx, Eli, Barney, Grigori — stand out precisely because nobody else does.

Two people, one city

John Patrick Lowrie voices the male citizens. Mary Kae Irvin voices the female citizens. That's it. Two performers are the entire population of City 17.

This is the same trick the series pulls everywhere once you notice it:

GroupHow the illusion is built
Black Mesa scientistsA handful of interchangeable models and performers become an entire research facility
MetropoliceHeavy radio filtering flattens individual voices into one police force
City 17 citizensTwo actors, a model pool, and random assembly become a city

In each case a severe technical constraint produced exactly the right dramatic effect. The scientists feel like staff, the police feel like a machine, and the citizens feel like a population — none of which a bigger voice cast would have delivered better.

Three lines worth stopping on

"I didn't see you get on."

Spoken on the train in the opening minutes, before you have a weapon or a clue. It reads as idle chatter. It isn't.

He didn't see you get on because you didn't. Gordon was placed there, moments earlier, by the G-Man. The game's first spoken line about the protagonist is a stranger noticing that the physics don't add up — and it's delivered by an anonymous NPC who never appears again.

"Don't drink the water. They put something in it to make you forget."

The most quoted line in the game, and the reason it works is that the game never returns to it. No document confirms it, no character repeats it, nothing in the plot depends on it.

So you're left with a stranger's claim you can't verify, in a city where verifying anything is impossible. That's a far more effective piece of world-building than a confirmed plot point would have been — and it's why people still argue about whether it's true.

"They have no reason to come to our place."
"Don't worry, they'll find one."

Two ambient lines, one exchange, thirteen words. It contains the entire logic of life under occupation: innocence is not protection, because the authority decides what counts as a reason.

Nobody is required to hear this. It plays in a room you might walk straight through. Half-Life 2 put its best writing in places where it could be missed, and trusted that the people who stopped to listen would be paid for it.

Finding the files yourself

Citizen audio lives under the games' sound/vo/ hierarchy, organised into folders per voice set — female01, male01 and so on — with the episodes keeping their own directories alongside the base game's.

We don't host the audio. These are Valve's copyrighted recordings. They're already in your installation if you own the games, and Valve's own tools are the intended way to browse them. This page publishes transcripts and context only.

If you're spawning citizens for testing or in a sandbox, npc_citizen is the entity, and its flags control which model and voice set gets used.

Frequently asked

Is Female 01 the same as Alyx?

No. Alyx Vance is a fully authored character with her own model, her own performer and her own dialogue. Female 01 is a generic citizen voice set.

How many citizen models are there?

Nine male and six female, with variations in clothing and condition — enough combinations that a crowd doesn't look copy-pasted at a glance.

Can citizens follow you?

Yes — in the later chapters you can recruit and lead squads of them, which changes their voice lines to squad acknowledgements and combat callouts.

Is this every citizen line?

No. Citizen dialogue is enormous and varies by chapter; this covers the City 17 material where the writing is densest. The games' own files are the complete reference.
