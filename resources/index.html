The Half-Life logo and the lambda symbol

ReferenceBranding & symbolismUpdated 2026

Half-Life's logo is one of the few in gaming that people can draw from memory, and one of even fewer that means something specific. The lambda isn't a stylistic choice that happened to look good — it's the equation in the title.

The physics

In radioactive decay, the lowercase Greek letter λ is the standard symbol for the decay constant — how fast a substance decays. A sample shrinks according to:

N(t) = N₀ · e−λt

The half-life — the time for half the sample to decay — comes straight out of that same constant:

t½ = ln(2) / λ

So "half-life" and "λ" are two ways of writing the same physical fact. Naming the game Half-Life and marking it with a lambda isn't a title plus a logo; it's one idea expressed twice, once in words and once in notation.

That's why the logo works. A random Greek letter would just be a shape. This one is the title restated in the language the game's protagonist actually thinks in — a theoretical physicist's shorthand, used as a brand.

The wordmark

The logo takes the obvious next step: the lambda doesn't sit beside the word, it replaces a letter inside it. Because λ resembles a capital A with an open leg, it slots into the "A" of HALF-LIFE and the word remains readable.

The result is a wordmark that carries its own symbol. Crop the lambda out and use it alone — on a crate, a wall, a boot screen — and people still read "Half-Life". Very few game logos survive that operation, which is why this one has lasted through a series, a sequel with a different visual identity, and twenty-five years of merchandise.

The two games treat it differently in tone. Half-Life 1's presentation is warm and industrial; Half-Life 2's is colder and more clinical, matching a game about occupation rather than accident. The mark itself doesn't change — the treatment does.

Lambda inside the game

The symbol does real work in Half-Life 2, where it becomes the mark of the Lambda Resistance — spray-painted at supply caches and along routes the resistance wants you to take.

It's a neat piece of design. The player already recognises the symbol from the box, so it needs no explanation the first time it appears on a wall, and it teaches a navigational rule with zero instruction: lambda means someone friendly was here. The brand becomes a gameplay signal without a tutorial line being spoken.

About downloading the logo

We don't host Valve's logo files. The Half-Life logo and the stylised lambda mark are trademarks of Valve Corporation. This site doesn't distribute them, and anywhere offering a "logo pack" is redistributing someone else's trademark.

Two things worth separating, though, because people conflate them:

  • The Greek letter is just a letter. λ is a standard character — you can type it, style it, and use it in your own work freely. It appears in mathematics, physics and half the programming languages in use.
  • Valve's specific logo design is a trademark. The particular lambda treatment, the wordmark, and the associated branding belong to Valve.

For legitimate uses — press coverage, storefront listings — Valve's own materials are the correct source. For a personal project, the letter itself is available to everyone and always was.

Frequently asked

Is the Half-Life 1 logo different from Half-Life 2's?

The lambda is constant across both; the surrounding treatment, colour and typography differ to match each game's tone. The recognisable element doesn't change, which is the point of it.

What font does Half-Life use?

The wordmark is custom lettering rather than an off-the-shelf typeface, which is normal for game logos and why lookalike fonts never quite match. Anything sold as "the Half-Life font" is an imitation.

Does the lambda mean anything else in the games?

Its main in-world role is as the Lambda Resistance mark. It also carries the same decay-constant association the title does, which is doing quiet thematic work throughout both games.

Can I use a lambda in my own mod or fan project?

The letter, yes — it belongs to Greek, not Valve. Reproducing Valve's specific logo design is a different question, and fan projects generally stay on the right side of it by using the letter rather than copying the wordmark.
