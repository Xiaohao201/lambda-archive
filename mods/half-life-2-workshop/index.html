The Half-Life 2 Steam Workshop — how it works and what it replaces

Mod guideHalf-Life 2 (Source)Updated 2026

For twenty years, modding Half-Life 2 meant downloading an archive, extracting it into the right folder, and restarting Steam. That changed in November 2024, and a lot of guides still haven't caught up.

Half-Life 2 has Steam Workshop support. It arrived with the 20th Anniversary Update on 16 November 2024. You can subscribe to community content and play it from inside the game — no folders, no manual extraction.

What the Workshop can do

Broader than people expect. Workshop content for Half-Life 2 covers:

  • Entire campaigns — full custom level sets, played through the base game.
  • Weapon replacements — new models, animations and effects.
  • Enemy replacements — reworked NPCs.
  • UI changes — HUD and menu overhauls.

The same update also brought Episode One, Episode Two and Lost Coast into Half-Life 2's own menu, so the whole series now sits under one installation rather than four library entries.

How to use it

01 Find the Workshop

Open Half-Life 2's Steam store page and select the Workshop tab, or reach it from the Community menu. Browse or search the same way you would for any other game with Workshop support.

02 Subscribe

Click Subscribe on an item. Steam downloads it automatically in the background — there is nothing to extract and no folder to find.

03 Enable it in-game

Launch Half-Life 2 and enable your subscribed content from the in-game menu. Campaigns are started from there; replacements apply to the game once active.

Unsubscribing removes the content cleanly. This is the Workshop's real advantage — no leftover files, no manual cleanup, no wondering which mod broke your install.

Replacements can conflict. Two items that replace the same weapon are in the same argument two GoldSrc gameplay mods have — enable one at a time when testing, and disable everything before reporting a problem.

Workshop vs the sourcemods folder

The Workshop did not replace the old system. Half-Life 2 now has two parallel mod systems, and knowing which one a mod uses saves a lot of confusion.

The rule of thumb: if a mod is big enough to feel like its own game, it's a sourcemod. Standalone total conversions with their own branding and menus still live in sourcemods and still need a Steam restart to appear.

Neither of these has anything to do with Half-Life 1. GoldSrc mods use a third layout entirely — see installing GoldSrc mods.

If your old mods stopped working

The 20th Anniversary Update changed Half-Life 2 substantially, and some existing mods don't run on the new build. This mirrors what happened to Half-Life 1 a year earlier.

Switching to the pre-anniversary build

  1. Right-click Half-Life 2 in your Steam library › Properties.
  2. Open the Betas tab.
  3. Select steam_legacy — described as Pre-20th Anniversary Build.

Don't confuse the two legacy branches. Half-Life 1 and Half-Life 2 each have a branch named steam_legacy, and they roll back to different things — Half-Life 1's is the Pre-25th Anniversary Build, Half-Life 2's is Pre-20th. Setting one does nothing for the other. See the full explanation of both.

Before rolling back, check whether the mod has an updated version — the anniversary build is the one Valve continues to support, and staying current is better when it's an option.

Frequently asked

Is Workshop content free?

Yes. Half-Life 2's Workshop is community content, subscribed at no cost.

Can I use Workshop mods and sourcemods at the same time?

They're separate systems, so yes in the sense that both can be installed. But a sourcemod is its own game entry — your Half-Life 2 Workshop subscriptions don't carry into it.

Do Workshop mods disable achievements?

Behaviour varies by item and Valve hasn't documented a blanket rule. If achievements matter to you, check the item's description or test with a save you don't mind losing.

Where does Workshop content download to?

Into Steam's Workshop content directory, managed automatically. You don't need to know the path — that's the point of it.

Can I publish my own mod to the Workshop?

Yes, that's what the Workshop is for. Valve's own developer documentation covers the packaging and submission process.
