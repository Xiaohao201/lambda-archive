Cat-Life — the Half-Life mod where you play as a cat

Mod profileHalf-Life (GoldSrc)Updated 2026

If you're looking for "the male cat Half-Life mod" or "the one where you're a cat in Black Mesa", this is it: Cat-Life GS. It's a single-player modification for Half-Life 1 that replaces Gordon Freeman with a house cat, and it is a much stranger and more thought-through project than that description suggests.

In one line: the Black Mesa incident from the floor, as Newton — a scientist's pet — with claws, teeth, night vision, and no way to open a door like a person.

The premise

You play as Newton, catalogued inside Black Mesa as project XFC-71 — a scientist's pet, and by implication a subject of his work.

When the resonance cascade happens, your owner dies. Nobody is coming to collect you. You're a cat inside a collapsing research facility full of things that eat animals your size, and the entire mod is about getting out.

What makes it work is that it doesn't reskin Gordon Freeman. Half-Life's other mods generally hand you a different arsenal; this one takes the arsenal away and rebuilds the game around what a cat actually has.

How it plays without weapons

Your toolkit is a cat's:

AbilityWhat it changes
Claws and teethYour only offence. Melee, short range, no ammunition to manage — and no way to deal with anything at a distance.
Night visionA cat sees in the dark, so unlit sections become traversable rather than blocking. Darkness stops being a hazard and starts being cover.
A cat's agilityRoutes a person couldn't take — vents, ledges, gaps, under things instead of through them.
Being smallCuts both ways. Spaces open up that Gordon could never use; enemies that were minor threats to an armoured scientist are now genuinely dangerous.

The result reframes familiar geometry. Black Mesa's corridors were built for a man in a HEV suit with a crowbar. Moving through the same space at knee height, unable to open doors or press buttons the normal way, turns level design you know into a puzzle.

Enemies and NPCs

The enemy roster is deliberately narrow, and the reasoning behind it is the most interesting design decision in the mod.

Confirmed enemies:

  • Headcrabs — roughly cat-sized, which makes them a fair fight for the first time in the series. In the base game they're a nuisance; here they're a peer.
  • Houndeyes — pack animals with an area attack, and a serious problem for something with no ranged option at all.

What you won't be fighting: Black Mesa's larger aliens and the military. This is intentional. A cat with claws cannot meaningfully fight an alien grunt, and putting one in front of you would make the mod unplayable rather than hard. The developers scaled the bestiary to the player character instead of inheriting Half-Life's.

Why this matters: most "play as X" mods keep the original enemies and become either trivial or impossible. Cat-Life rebuilt the threat scale to match what you actually are, which is why it works as a game rather than a novelty.

Your owner — the scientist — is dead before the mod begins. He's the reason you're alone, not a companion you travel with.

What "GS" means

GoldSource — the engine Half-Life 1 runs on, usually shortened to GoldSrc. The suffix identifies which engine the project targets, and it's why Cat-Life GS installs like any other Half-Life 1 mod rather than a Source mod.

Practically, that means it goes in its own folder inside your Half-Life directory, not in sourcemods. If you're unclear on that distinction, our mods overview lays out both layouts side by side.

How to get it

Cat-Life is distributed through its ModDB page, which is where the authors post releases and updates. A playable demo has been released, with the full project planned around six chapters plus a new training course.

Check the current release state before downloading. Long-running GoldSrc projects change status, and the ModDB page is the authoritative source for what's actually available right now — not a guide like this one.

You'll need an installed copy of Half-Life 1. Like every GoldSrc mod, Cat-Life supplies its own content but runs on the base game's engine and assets.

If it doesn't start, the usual GoldSrc causes apply — engine version, folder nesting, launch parameters. Our GoldSrc troubleshooting walkthrough covers all of them in order.

Frequently asked

Is this the same as the "Maxwell the Cat" mod?

No. Those are joke mods that swap a model or NPC for the Maxwell meme. Cat-Life is a purpose-built single-player campaign with its own mechanics, levels and enemy design.

Can I play Cat-Life without owning Half-Life?

No. It's a GoldSrc mod and needs an installed copy of Half-Life 1 to run.

Does it work after the 25th Anniversary update?

That update broke a large share of GoldSrc mods. If it won't start, the standard fix is to switch Half-Life to the steam_legacy beta branch — see our troubleshooting guide.

Are there other "play as an animal" Half-Life mods?

A few exist, but Cat-Life is by far the best known and the most developed. Most others are experiments or model swaps rather than full campaigns.
