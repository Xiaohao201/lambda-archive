Half-Life PS2: differences, Decay and exclusive content

Platform historyPlayStation 2, 2001Updated August 2026

What is different about Half-Life on PS2? The 2001 PlayStation 2 port adds the exclusive Decay co-op campaign, upgraded character models, console-specific controls and an unlockable alien mission. Its biggest addition has never received an official PC release.

The headline: Half-Life: Decay, a two-player co-operative campaign built for the port by Gearbox Software, released November 2001, and never officially released on PC in the twenty-five years since.

Decay — the exclusive campaign

Decay is a full co-operative campaign covering the Black Mesa incident from yet another angle, and it's built around two players rather than adapted for them.

  • Split-screen co-op. Two players, one television, 2001-style.
  • Solo play by character switching. Without a second player you swap between the two protagonists — which turns co-op puzzles into single-player puzzles about positioning.
  • Genuinely co-operative design. Puzzles are built around two people being in different places, not one person doing everything twice.

That last point is why Decay still gets talked about. Co-op in 2001 usually meant "the same campaign, with a friend." Decay's problems require two positions at once.

Gina Cross and Colette Green

The two playable characters are Black Mesa scientists — Dr. Gina Cross and Dr. Colette Green — living through the same catastrophe from their own corner of the facility.

This makes Decay the third alternate-perspective story on the Black Mesa incident:

GameYou playPlatform
Half-LifeGordon Freeman, research associatePC and PS2
Opposing ForceAdrian Shephard, HECU marinePC
Blue ShiftBarney Calhoun, security guardPC
DecayGina Cross and Colette Green, scientistsPS2 only

Worth noting what that table shows: Half-Life's expansions consistently ask the same question — what was everyone else doing while Gordon was busy? — and Decay is the only one that answers it with two people at once, and the only one most players have never been able to play.

It's also the only entry in that list with women as the protagonists, which in a 1998–2001 first-person shooter lineup was not the norm.

What else the port changed

The PS2 version wasn't a straight conversion. Gearbox rebuilt assets to take advantage of hardware that arrived three years after the original:

ChangeWhat it did
Higher-detail character modelsRebuilt rather than upscaled
Eyes that track movementNPCs look at things — including you
More detailed facial expressionsFaces that do more than exist
Individually jointed fingersHands that articulate instead of being a single mitten shape
Modified campaignLevel adjustments for console memory and controls
New multiplayer modesBeyond the PC deathmatch offering

Tracking eyes and articulated fingers in 2001 is the detail worth pausing on. Those are exactly the things Half-Life 2 would build its whole facial animation system around three years later. The PS2 port is a visible step between the two games.

The unlockable alien mission

The port also hides a bonus mission that flips the perspective entirely: you play as one of the creatures you spend the rest of the game shooting.

It's short and it's a novelty, but it fits the series' habit of handing you someone else's point of view — the same instinct behind Opposing Force, Blue Shift, and eventually fan projects that take it much further.

Playing it today

There's no clean answer, and it's worth being straight about that.

RouteReality
Original PS2 hardware Works, and is the only official way. Requires a console, a disc, and a display that accepts its output.
Buying the disc Still circulates second-hand. It's a 2001 console game, priced accordingly.
Official digital release Does not exist. Decay has never been sold digitally on any platform.
Community PC ports Fans have recreated Decay for PC over the years. Unofficial, and not endorsed or distributed by Valve.

Don't expect this on Steam. Decay has been absent for twenty-five years, including through the 25th Anniversary update — which added Uplink and restored other archival content but not this. Whatever the reason, it hasn't changed.

Frequently asked

Is the PS2 version worth playing over the PC version?

For the main campaign, no — the PC version is better in every way and has received twenty-five years of updates. For Decay, the PS2 version is the only official option that exists.

Can you play Decay alone?

Yes, by switching between the two characters. It changes the feel — co-op puzzles become positioning puzzles — but it's a supported way to play, not a workaround.

Is Decay canon?

It's an officially developed and published Gearbox expansion, same as Opposing Force and Blue Shift. Whether any of the expansions are strictly canon is a longer argument the series has never settled.

Did Half-Life come to other consoles?

A Dreamcast version was developed and cancelled before release — its assets leaked and circulate among preservationists. The PS2 release is the one that actually shipped.

Does the PS2 version have mods?

No. GoldSrc modding depends on the PC file structure and the ability to swap libraries — see installing GoldSrc mods for why that doesn't translate to a console disc.
