Headcrabs — every variant, and what each one creates

Creature profileHalf-Life 1 & 2Updated 2026

The headcrab is the most recognisable thing Half-Life invented, and the reason isn't that it's frightening. It's that every zombie in the series is legible — you can look at one and read what happened to it.

The count people usually get wrong: there are three headcrab types and four zombie types. The Zombine is not a fourth headcrab — it's a standard headcrab that found a different kind of host.

The three headcrabs

TypeFirst seenBehaviour
Standard Half-Life 1 Slow but strong, killing with its claws. Moans almost constantly and growls when it detects prey — which is why you always hear them before you see them. In Half-Life 2 they knock loose objects around as they move, turning debris into incidental projectiles.
Fast Half-Life 2 Long-legged and the quickest of the three. Trades the standard's durability for speed, and gets on you before you finish identifying it.
Poison Half-Life 2 Slow, venomous, and poisonous. Delivers a neurotoxin by bite — and is lethal to predators that eat it, which is a piece of ecology the game never needed to include.

Note the design logic: the standard is a baseline threat, the fast one attacks your reaction time, and the poison one attacks your health economy. Three enemies, three different things being tested.

The four zombies

A headcrab that reaches a host produces a zombie, and which zombie you get depends on which headcrab arrived:

Zombie= Headcrab+ Host

ZombineStandardOverwatch soldier

That last row is the interesting one, and it does real work in the story.

The Zombine

A standard headcrab takes a Combine soldier. The result keeps enough of the soldier's training to be a different problem entirely: a Zombine will pull the pin on a live grenade and charge you with it, killing itself to reach you.

Mechanically it's an enemy that forces movement — you can't hold a corridor against something that wants to detonate in it.

Thematically it's doing something sharper. The Combine soldiers spend two games as the faceless, disciplined, apparently post-human enemy. The Zombine establishes that they're meat with equipment on — susceptible to exactly the same parasite as the civilians they police. Half-Life 2 makes that argument by putting a headcrab on one, and never says a word about it.

Why the design works

Most shooters have zombies. Half-Life's are unusual because the cause is still attached to the effect.

  • The enemy carries its own evidence. You can see the headcrab on the head. There's no infection lore to explain — the explanation is standing in front of you, still gripping someone's face.
  • The victim stays visible. These are recognisably people wearing the clothes they died in. In Half-Life 1 they're wearing lab coats and security uniforms — the same outfits worn by the scientists you've been escorting.
  • It kills the headcrab and the victim separately. Shoot the crab off and what falls over is a corpse you can identify.
  • It scales without new fiction. New headcrab, new zombie, no explanation required. The system generates enemies for free.

The compact version: a headcrab zombie is a sentence with the subject and object both still present. Almost every other zombie in games is just a monster.

Fighting them

Practical notes, since these are the enemies you'll meet most:

  • The crowbar handles standard headcrabs. Spending ammunition on them is the classic new-player mistake — see why Half-Life 1 feels so hard.
  • Listen. They moan and growl constantly. In Half-Life you almost always get audio warning before visual contact.
  • Fast headcrabs need a different weapon. Melee against something faster than you is a losing proposition.
  • Poison headcrabs are a priority target. Kill them before they reach you; the recovery afterwards is worse than the fight.
  • Give Zombines room. The moment you hear the grenade, the correct move is distance, not damage.

Frequently asked

Are there headcrabs in Half-Life 1?

Yes — the standard type, from the opening chapters onward. Fast and poison headcrabs are Half-Life 2 additions.

Can a headcrab take Gordon Freeman?

No. They damage you and can kill you, but the game never zombifies the player. The HEV suit is the in-fiction reason usually given; the design reason is that it would end the game.

Where do headcrabs come from?

Xen originally, arriving through the resonance cascade in Half-Life 1. By Half-Life 2 the Combine deploy them deliberately as a weapon, which is a considerably darker use of the same creature.

Is the Zombine in Half-Life 2 itself?

It appears in the episodic content rather than the base game, which is why players who only finished Half-Life 2 sometimes haven't met one.
