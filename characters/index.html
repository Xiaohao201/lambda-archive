Half-Life characters

Section indexHalf-Life 1 & 2Updated 2026

Half-Life has a small cast for a series this influential, and that's deliberate. You spend most of both games alone. The people you do meet get remembered precisely because there aren't many of them — and because the one at the centre never says a word.

Half-Life 1

CharacterWho they are
Gordon Freeman You. A theoretical physicist at Black Mesa who was holding the sample when everything went wrong. Never speaks, never appears in a cutscene, never has a reaction you didn't supply yourself.
The G-Man A man in a blue suit with a briefcase, appearing at the edges of levels, watching. Never explained. His speech pattern — stresses on the wrong syllables, pauses in the wrong places — does most of the work of making him unsettling.
Black Mesa scientists The facility's staff, and the game's main source of information and comedy. Built from a handful of interchangeable models, which makes them read as a workforce rather than a cast.
Security guards The other survivors. Will follow you, will fight for you, will die doing it. One of them later gets a name and a sequel.
Vortigaunts Enslaved alien labourers, enemies in the first game and allies later — a reversal the series does more thoughtfully than most.
Headcrabs Not characters, but the series' signature creature and the reason a quiet room is never actually quiet. Three variants, four zombies.
Nihilanth What's on the other side of the portal, and the first game's final obstacle.

Half-Life 2

CharacterWho they are
Alyx Vance The series' most significant addition — a companion who is competent, funny, and never needs escorting. Much of what people remember about Half-Life 2 is her.
Eli Vance Alyx's father and a Black Mesa survivor. One of the few people who knows what actually happened.
Dr. Wallace Breen Formerly Black Mesa's administrator, now the Combine's human administrator. His broadcasts are the game's most sustained piece of writing.
Dr. Isaac Kleiner Scientist, Black Mesa survivor, and the source of most of the game's warmth.
Barney Calhoun The security guard from the first game, now embedded in Civil Protection. Still owes you a beer.
Father Grigori Ravenholm's remaining resident. Appears briefly, remembered permanently.
Dog Alyx's robot. Built by her father to protect her, and characterised almost entirely through movement.
Metropolice Civil Protection — human collaborators policing City 17. Their voice lines are archived in full.

The silent protagonist

Gordon Freeman's silence is the series' defining formal decision, and it's more radical than it looks.

Other characters don't ignore him — they address him directly, ask him questions, and pause as though he answered. The gap is deliberate and you fill it. The trick only works because the game never once cuts away to show you what Gordon did; there is no version of him on screen contradicting the one in your head.

It's the reason a character with no dialogue, no face, and no expressed opinions is one of the most recognisable protagonists in the medium.

Frequently asked

Who is the G-Man working for?

Never stated. He refers to employers without naming them, and the games are careful never to resolve it. Two decades of theories exist; none are confirmed, and the ambiguity is clearly the intent.

Is Barney in Half-Life 1?

The security guard model is, and Blue Shift retroactively made one of them Barney Calhoun and gave him his own campaign. He returns as a named character in Half-Life 2.

Does Gordon Freeman ever speak?

Not once, in any game.
